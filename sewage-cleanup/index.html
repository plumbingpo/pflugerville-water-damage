A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Pflugerville, Pflugerville Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
